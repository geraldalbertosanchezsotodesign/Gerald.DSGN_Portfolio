Non sei sol*

This manual was born from a simple question I asked myself when we were assigned the project:
"What manual would I have needed when I was little?"

As a child, I lived in a home with an abusive father. I witnessed and experienced physical abuse.

From that memory came the idea to create something that didn't exist for me at the time:
a manual designed for children experiencing the same situation.

“Non sei sol* (You aren't alone)” it's not a neutral project. It's a necessary act.

The voice I wished I'd heard


I chose to build a narrator's voice that spoke directly to the child, not about them, not around them.

A voice that came from someone who had lived through that situation, and could say with credibility: I was there too, I felt what you're feeling.

The tone leans on psychology not as a distant discipline, but as a concrete tool for naming things and that's because understanding the behavior of someone who hurts you doesn't justify it, but it removes you from the equation. It tells you that you are not the cause.

For me, discovering that was everything.

I wanted the manual to be a moment of stillness. A place where a child could sit with their own thoughts, recognize them, and find something of themselves outside of fear.

A book that doesn't hide


I built the visual system around three colors: black, white, and a desaturated shade of blue I chose because it communicated lightness and melancholy at once.

It is no coincidence that it is also the color of Telefono Azzurro, the emergency helpline for domestic violence. A silent reference, but a present one.

The illustrations draw from the aesthetic of Saul Bass, geometric and essential, but I wanted them imperfect, as if a child had tried to trace figures onto translucent paper.

That imperfection is not a flaw: it is the signature of someone still learning how to see the world.
