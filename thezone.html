The Zone

The objective was to design a comprehensive 74-page editorial project with full creative freedom.

The primary focus was the development and rigorous application of a custom grid system, a core competency I mastered through and thanks to this course.

In this case I chose to mix two of my greatest passions, namely sports and human psychology, through graphic design.

Where the body stops thinking


I chose the Zone because it belongs to everyone, the beauty of this subject is that sport is only the most visible way to describe it.

At its core, it has far more to do with mental concentration than with physical ability: that moment when the mind stops interfering and simply lets things happen. Athletes call it Flow.

Creatives call it the creative current.

I chose a theme that could move freely between both worlds, and it kept me engaged for the entire duration of the project.

Where decisions become form


I built the grid on a 10pt type size with 12pt leading, balanced margins on three sides and a generous 83pt bottom that grounds the page. A 15pt gutter keeps the columns clean without pushing them apart.

For typography, two families in contrast: Neue Haas Grotesk, geometric, direct, modern, carries the structural voice of the book. Big Caslon CC enters where the content needs weight and stillness. A shift that mirrors the Zone itself: the tension between motion and control.

The hardest challenge in this project process was maintaining visual consistency across the whole book. InDesign's preview mode misled me more than once, and the print phase arrived last, with the full weight of every definitive choice.

What kept me on course were the periodic reviews with Professor Venturi: direct, precise, always pointing to the problem without handing me the solution.

An aesthetic with no time and no space


I built the visual system around three colors: black, white, and teal, because together they construct something almost dreamlike: a state of control, concentration, and mental power.

This is not a decorative palette. It is a feeling.

The images were generated using AI, each chapter following its own visual style, one for each phase of the Zone. What holds them together is not form but atmosphere: the color, the timeless tension between a body in motion and the space around it.

I paired each chapter with a different paper stock: the Doing chapter printed on pearlescent paper, the Being chapter on teal-colored cardstock. A choice you feel before you even read.
