Noise Visual Identity

For the Brand Design exam, we had to choose one of the businesses present in the area where our academy is located and develop its visual identity, ending by inserting it into a brand manual.

This Brand Manual defines identity, values, tone of voice, and visual system not as constraints, but as tools for a consistent and evolving Visual Identity.

Teamwork is Dreamwork...


When Elena Furio and I started working on NOISE, we didn't know each other. The first days were marked by small clashes that arose from the different ways of understanding the project and the working method, which is only natural when two people are still figuring out how to move in the same space.

But that's exactly where the division of roles emerged from: Elena led the brand analysis and research, while I took on creative direction and the construction of the visual system.

Two different perspectives that, instead of colliding, locked into place, her meticulous reading of the context gave creative space and depth to my visual choices, and vice versa.

The result was a project stronger than either of us would have built alone, and the top academic result we achieved is the clearest proof of that.

Cultural Noise and Visual Noise


I developed the concept around a direct parallel between two forms of noise.

Visual noise is crafted through optical illusion patterns and modulated line work is not merely an aesthetic device. It is the graphic translation of the cultural noise NOISE aims to generate: an initiative designed to disrupt visual habits and disorient the viewer, redirecting focus toward underground and independent cultural space.

The lines shaping the "Optical Flow" reflect the exact cinema NOISE champions: work that vibrates, resists immediate consumption, and actively sparks dialogue, debate, and culture...

Dynamic Identity


To make the identity truly responsive across diverse touchpoints, I modulated the stroke profile of the "Optical Flow," varying line width and density to generate six distinct variants tailored to specific events and media contexts.

This approach sits at the intersection of a purely generative system where patterns could theoretically regenerate infinitely and a controlled dynamic identity, ensuring every iteration remains unmistakably part of the same visual family.

The result is a responsive mark that adapts without losing coherence: an identity that shifts its skin across contexts while preserving its core character.

Book Binding


A brand manual isn't an object you read once and file away: it moves from hand to hand, crosses different graphic departments, gets opened, consulted, flipped through quickly during a briefing.

That's why Swiss binding was a functional choice before it was an aesthetic one; it allows the manual to lie completely flat and withstand repeated, not-so-gentle use. The cover, in 500g black cardstock, provides the sturdiness needed for a document built to last.

Inside, the reinforced naked pages sewn with black thread aren't just a technical detail: the exposed stitching physically echoes "Optical Flow," the modulated line system at the core of NOISE's identity, carrying the brand's language all the way into the object's physical structure.

Printing Techniques


The black paste cover, printed in white varnish, is the detail that elevates the manual from a straightforward technical tool to a fine editorial object.

The contrast between the matte black surface and the glossy, slightly raised texture of the varnish creates a tactile reading before a visual one, it's an invitation to touch the manual before opening it.

It's a printing technique designed to elevate the perception of the project, giving NOISE's brand an underground, essential direction, a physical form coherent with its identity, where every production detail tells the same story as the graphics themselves.
